# ICS Overview

## What is ICS?

- The **Incident Command System** is standard for IM used by orgs and govts for small emergencies, large emergencies, and planned events

- By using the standard, all groups can work together more efficiently and share resources with greater ease

---

## What does ICS do for incident management?

- Provides a systematic planning process to prepare for incidents
--

- Implements a common, flexible, and predesigned management structure
--

- Fosters cooperation between disciplines and agencies
--

- Clarifies chain of command and supervision responsibilities, improving accountability
--

- Leverages interoperable communications systems and plain language, improving communications
--

---

# NIMS Overview

## What is NIMS?

- The **National Incident Management System** is a framework for incident management, meant to be usable regardless of cause, size, location, or complexity

- It provides the procedures and organization for incident response teams to handle an incident
--

  - It does not provide actual resources or a specific communications plan

---

## NIMS

### Guiding Principles

1. Flexibility
2. Standardization
3. Unity of Effort

### Major Components

1. Resource Management
2. Command and Coordination
3. Communications and Information Management

---

### Guiding Principle: Flexibility

- NIMS should be usable for any incident
--

  - A fairly trivial local incident
--

  - A major incident requiring interstate mutual aid or even federal assistance
--

- NIMS should be applicable regardless of hazard, geography, demographics, climate, culture, or organizational authority structure
--

- NIMS should be usable for planned events (e.g., concerts), forecasted events (e.g., hurricanes), and events with little or no notice (e.g., tornadoes)

---

### Guiding Principle: Standardization

- NIMS defines **standard organizational structures** that improve integration and connectivity among involved organizations
--

- NIMS defines **standard practices** that allow incident personnel and organizations to work together effectively
--

- NIMS includes **common terminology** to enable effective communication

---

### Guiding Principle: Unity of Effort

- Unity of Effort simply means coordinating activities among various groups to achieve common objectives
--

- Organizations with jurisdictional authority or functional responsibilities should support each other while also allowing other participating agencies to maintain their own authorities and accountabilities

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Identifying and typing resources

- Resource typing defines and categorizes resources by capability

- Establishes common definitions for the capabilities of resources

- Types definitions:

  - **Capability**: the resource's capability to perform its function in one or more of the five mission areas (Prevention, Protection, Mitigation, Response, Recovery)
  - **Category**: the function for which a resource would be most useful (e.g., firefighting, law enforcement, health, medical, communications)
  - **Kind**: a broad characterization (e.g., personnel, equipment, team, facility)
  - **Type**: level of capability to perform its function based on size, power, capacity, experience, qualifications (Type 1 has the greatest capability, Type 4 the smallest)

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Qualifying, certifying, and credentialing personnel

- The AHJ, or Authority Having Jurisdiction, is responsible for qualification, certification, and credentialing within an organization/jurisdiction

- **Qualifying**: personnel can meet the minimum established standards (training, experience, physical and medical fitness) to fill specific positions

- **Certification**: recognition from an AHJ or a third party that an individual has completed qualification for a position (e.g., state board for a medical professional)

- **Credentialing**: the documentation that identifies personnel and verifies their qualifications (e.g., an ID card or badge)

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Planning for resources

- Jurisdictions and organizations work together *before* incidents to develop plans identifying, managing, estimating, allocating, ordering, deploying, and demobilizing resources

- This includes identifying resource requirements for anticipated threats and vulnerabilities

- RM planning should consider resources for all mission areas: Prevention, Protection, Mitigation, Response, Recovery

- Planners should consider stockpiling resources, establishing mutual aid agreements, determining how and where to assign resources performing non-essential tasks, and developing contracts with vendors for resource 
acquisition

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Acquiring, storing, and inventorying resources

- Organizations acquire, store, and inventory resources as a normal day-to-day activity

- Effective resource management requires a current and accurate inventory of resources, whether it's as simple as a spreadsheet on a sheet of paper or a fancy computer inventory system

- As far as NIMS is concerned, resource inventorying refers to preparedness activities performed outside of incident response

  - Resource *tracking* occurs during an incident

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Tasks during an incident

1. Identify requirements
--

2. Order and acquire
--

3. Mobilize
--

4. Track and report
--

5. Demobilize
--

6. Reimburse and restock

---

### Major Component: Resource Management

#### Mutual aid agreements

- Mutual aid agreements establish the legal basis for two or more entities to share resources

- When receiving a request for mutual aid, the supporting jurisdiction determines if it would still be able to meet its own requirements during the temporary loss of resources

- EMAC (Emergency Management Assistance Compact) is a mutual aid compact ratified by Congress

  - Defines a non-federal state-to-state system for sharing resources across state lines during an emergency or disaster

  - Signed by all 50 states, DC, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the US Virgin Islands

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Common Terminology

- ICS gives known names and definitions to major functions, units, and resources

- All communication should use those names and should avoid:

  - Jargon
  
  - Acronyms
  
  - Radio and agency-specific codes

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Modular Organization

- The structure of the incident response organization is modular

- It is designed to be expanded and contracted as needed

- The responsibility for establishing and modifying the module organization belongs with the **Incident Commander**

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Management by Objectives

- The **Incident Commander** or **Unified Command** is responsible for establishing incident objectives

  - These drive operations/activities done to handle the incident

- **Management by Objectives** includes:

  - Establishing specific and measurable objectives

  - Identifying strategies and tasks

  - Developing and issuing assignments and plans/procedures/protocols

  - Documenting the results of those objectives

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Incident Action Planning

- An **Incident Action Plan** (IAP) is a concise document capturing and communicating priorities, objectives, and assignments

- IAP should focus on addressing the needs for an "operational period"

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Incident Action Planning

- The desired characteristics of an IAP are:

  1. Cover a specific timeframe
  2. Be proactive
  3. Specify the incident objectives
  4. State the activities to be completed
  5. Assign responsibilities
  6. Identify needed resources
  7. Specify communication protocols

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Incident Action Planning

- Small incidents may have an IAP that is oral or written

- Large incidents must have a written IAP

- Any hazardous materials incident, regardless of size, requires an IAP

- ICS Form 202 (Incident Objectives) helps develop a written IAP

  - Form 202 is available in Winlink

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Manageable Span of Control

- **Span of control** is the number of resources that a single supervisor can effectively manage during an incident

- As a general guideline, the optimal span of control for one supervisor is between 1 and 5 (1:5) resources


---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Incident Facilities and Locations

- Incident Command may establish designated facilities

- Some common support facilities are:

  - Incident Command Post (ICP)
  - Incident base, staging areas, and camps
  - Mass casualty triage areas
  - Point-of-distribution
  - Emergency shelters

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Comprehensive Resource Management

- **Resource Management** (RM) is managing any sort of resource (e.g., people, teams, facilities, equipment, or supplies)

- Includes identifying resources, ordering and acquiring them, mobilization, tracking and reporting, demobilization, and reimbursement or restocking

- Managing resources may include ensuring resource qualification and credentialing the resources

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Integrated Communications

- Proper incident management includes ensuring that everyone involved in managing the incident can communicate

- Voice and data links need to stay connected/available

- Ensure that those involved have the necessary situational awareness

- Allow information to be shared between groups as needed

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Establishment and Transfer of Command

- Establishing command is one of the first tasks to be completed

- The jurisdiction or organization with primary responsibility designates an **Incident Commander** and defines the process to transfer command

- If command needs to be transferred, the process should include a briefing to ensure the new command has all essential information

  - This allows incident response to continue to be done safely and effectively

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Unified Command

- A large incident may involve multiple jurisdictions or require the authority or resources of multiple groups to manage

- In that case, rather than a single Incident Commander, a **Unified Command** is established to jointly manage the incident

- Groups with different legal, functional, or geographic responsibilities work together without affecting the authority, responsibility, or accountability of the individual organizations

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Chain of Command and Unity of Command

##### Chain of Command

- **Chain of command** is an *orderly line* showing how authority flows through the incident's organizational structure

- Allows the Incident Commander to direct and control the actions of all personnel

- Requires that all orders flow from supervisors downward

  - It does **not** prevent individuals from speaking to others and sharing information, just from providing orders

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Chain of Command and Unity of Command

##### Unity of Command

- **Unity of command** means that all individuals have a single designated supervisor to report to

- All work assignments should come from that single ICS supervisor

- The command structure of the ICS organization may differ from the administrative structure of the jurisdiction/agency the individual belongs to

  - When assigned to an incident, they report to their ICS supervisor, **not** their day-to-day supervisor

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Accountability

- All individuals must abide by all of the following (where applicable):

  - Agency policies and guidelines
  - Local laws and regulations
  - Tribal laws and regulations
  - State laws and regulations
  - Federal laws and regulations

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Accountability

##### Principles

- **Check-in/check-out**: All responders must check in to receive an assignment, and check out upon departure.

- **Incident Action Planning**: Operations must be coordinated following the IAP.

- **Unity of Command**: Each individual is assigned to just one supervisor.

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Accountability

##### Principles

- **Personal Responsibility**: ICS relies on individuals taking personal accountability for their own actions.

- **Span of Control**: Supervisors must be able to effectively supervise, control, and communicate with the resources under their supervision.

- **Resource Tracking**: Supervisors must record and report resource status changes as soon as they occur. Accountability for a resource begins when the resource is requested and continues until the resource returns to their home base safely.

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Dispatch/Deployment

- Only be deployed when requested/dispatched by an appropriate authority

- This should be done through established RM systems (e.g., pagers)

- Resources must not self-dispatch

  - This can overburder incident command

  - It also means that the resource may be needed elsewhere and is no longer available

---

### Major Component: Command and Coordination

#### Information and Intelligence Management

- Incident management needs to establish a process to gather, analyze/assess, share, and manage information and intelligence related to the incident

- In NIMS, *intelligence* exclusively refers to threat-related information from law enforcement, medical surveillance, and other investigative organizations

- Information and intelligence may come from many sources

- Example sources: 

  - 911 calls, radio/video/data communications by responders, situation reports, technical specialists, reports from field observers, geospatial data (GIS), media, risk assessments, terroristic/violent threats, surveillance of disease outbreaks, structural plans and vulnerabilities

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

- Effective incident management requires communications and information systems that provide timely, accurate, and relevant information

- Four key principles:

  - Interoperability
  - Reliability, scalability, and portability
  - Resilience and redundancy
  - Security

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Principle: Interoperability

- The capacity for emergency management and response personnel to interact and work well together
  - Within and across jurisdictions and organizations
  - Over voice, data, and video systems
  - In real time

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Principle: Reliability, scalability, and portability

- **Reliable**: familiar to users, adaptable to new technology, and dependable in any situation
- **Portable**: can be transported, deployed, and integrated easily to enable support of incidents across jurisdictions
- **Scalable**: can expand to support any incident, small or large, and support a rapid increase in the number of users

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Principle: Resilience and redundancy

- **Resiliency**: withstand and continue to perform after damage to or loss of infrastructure
- **Redundancy**: duplicate systems enable continuity through alternate communication methods when primary methods fail

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Principle: Security

- Some incident information is sensitive, so communications systems should be able to be secured to the appropriate level to control access to sensitive/restricted information
- Communications and information sharing should also comply with data protection and privacy laws

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

- There are four standard communication types used by and within NIMS:

  - Strategic Communications
  - Tactical Communications
  - Support Communications
  - Public Communications

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Communication Types: Strategic

- High-level directions
  - Resource priority decisions
  - Roles and responsibilities determinations
  - Overall incident management courses of action

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Communication Types: Tactical

- Communications among and between on-scene command and tactical personnel and cooperating agencies and organization

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Communication Types: Support

-  Coordination of support of strategic and tactical communications
  - Communications among hospitals concerning resource ordering, dispatching, and tracking
  - Traffic and public works communications

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

#### Communication Types: Public

- Alerts and warnings
- Press conferences

---

### Major Component: Communications and Information Management

- Determines what systems/platforms are used, who can use the systems, what information is essential, and what the technical requirements are for equipment/systems
- These plans should be made into formal agreements and should include things such as the connection of separate networks, data format standards, and cybersecurity agreements
- All stakeholders should be involved in communications planning

---

## ICS Functional Areas

- There are five functional areas defined by ICS:

  1. Command
  2. Operations
  3. Planning
  4. Logistics
  5. Finance/administration

- NIMS also defines "Intelligence/Investigations"

  - Could be its own group, fall under Operations or Plannning, or fall to command staff reporting directly to the Incident Commander

---

## ICS Command Staff

- ICS does not dictate that each area be established as a **command staff** position

- Until the Incident Command delegates the functionality, the IC is directly responsible for an area's task

- When delegated, the person in charge of that section is the **Section Chief** and has the ability to expand the section to meet situational needs

- The only command staff position that is **always** staffed is IC

  - IC may be the entire response for a very small incident

---

## ICS Command Staff

- IC is responsible for:

  - Overall management of the incident

  - Ensuring overall incident safety

  - Providing information services to internal ande xternal stakeholders

  - Establishing and maintaining liaisons with other agencies participating in the response

- May appoint one or more deputy ICs (who should be fully qualified to assume the IC position)

---

## ICS Command Staff

- Additional staff roles for providing information, safety, and liaison services mthat may be created are:

  - **Public Information Officer**: Interface with the public and media and/or other agencies needing incident-related information
  - **Safety Officer**: Monitors incident operations and advises the IC on matters relating to safety, including personnel health and safety
  - **Liaison Officer**: Services as the IC's point of contact for representatives of government agencies, NGOs, and private-sector organizations

- IC may also appoint technical specialists to act as **command advisors**

  - Legal, medical, science/technology, access, and other functional needs

---

## ICS Command Staff Responsibilities

### Operations

  - Implements strategies and develops tactics to carry out incident objectives
  - Directing the management of all tactical activities on behalf of the IC
  - Supports the development of the IAP to ensure it accurately reflects current operations
  - Organizes, assigns, and supervises tactical response resources

---

## ICS Command Staff Responsibilities

### Planning

  - Prepares and documents IAPs
  - Manages information
  - Maintains situational awareness for the incident
  - Tracks resources assigned to the incident
  - Maintains incident documentation
  - Develops plans for demobilization

---

## ICS Command Staff Responsibilities

### Logistics

  - Orders, obtains, maintains, and accounts for essential personnel, equipment, and supplies
  - Provides communication planning and resources
  - Sets up food services for responders
  - Set up and maintains incident facilities
  - Provides support transportation
  - Provides medical services to incident personnel

---

## ICS Command Staff Responsibilities

### Finance/Administration

  - Contract negotiation and monitoring
  - Timekeeping
  - Cost analysis
  - Compensation for injuries or damages to property
  - Documentation for reimbursement (e.g., mutual aid agreements or assistance agreements)

---

## ICS Facilities

- Incident Command Post (ICP)
	- Location of the tactical-level, on-scene incident management (Incident Commander or Unified Command and Staff)
- Staging Areas
	- Accounts for personnel, supplies, and equipment awaiting assignment
- Incident Base
	- Location at which personnel conduct primary support activities
	- May be co-located with the ICP
- Camps
	- Satellites to an Incident Base
	- Established where they can best support incident operations by providing food, sleeping areas, sanitation, and minor maintenance and servicing of equipment

---

## Incident Management Teams

- IMTs are groups of ICS-qualified personnel

  - Incident Commander
  - Other incident leadership
  - Personnel qualified for other key ICS positions

- Established at local, regional, tribal, state, and national levels with notification, deployment, and operational procedures formally defined

- Typed based on team member qualifications

---

## Incident Management Teams

- When assigned to an incident, an IMT is typically delegated the authority to act on behalf of the affected jurisdiction/organization

- Some IMTs are referred to as IMATs (Incident Management Assistant Teams) as a clairification that they *support* on-scene personnel and/or the affected jurisdiction/organization

---

## Emegency Operations Centers (EOCs)

- An EOC is an off-site location where staff from multiple agencies join to address threats/hazards and provide coordinated support

- Purpose, authority, and composition vary, but typically the following functions are performed:

  - Collect, analyze, and share information
  - Support resource needs and requests (including allocation and tracking)
  - Coordinate plans and determine both current and future needs
  - Possibly provide coordination and policy direction

- Can be a fixed location, a temporary facility, or virtual

---

## Emegency Operations Centers (EOCs)

### NIMS' Common EOC Organizations

1. ICS or ICS-like structure
	1. Operations, Planning, Logistics, and Finance/Administration groups may exist under the EOC director
2. Incident Support Model structure
	1. The ISM structure is often used by groups that choose to focus their EOC on information, planning, and resource support
	2. Groups under the EOC director may include Situational Awareness, Planning Support, Resources Support, and Center Support
3. Departmental structure
	1. The departmental structure uses the day-to-day structure of the department/agency
	2. This structure has minimal preparation and startup time

---

## Emegency Operations Centers (EOCs)

### EOC Activation Levels

- 3: Normal Operations/Steady State
  - Activities that are normal for the EOC when no incident or specific risk or hazard has been identified
  - Routine watch and warning activities if the EOC normally houses this function
- 2: Enhanced Steady-State/Partial Activation
  - Certain EOC team members/organizations are activated to monitor a credible threat, risk, or hazard and/or to support the response to a new and potentially evolving incident
- 1: Full Activation
  - EOC team is activated, including personnel from all assisting agencies, to support the response to a major incident or credible threat

---

## Multiagency Coordination Groups (MAC Groups)

- MAC groups, or policy groups, are part of the NIMS off-site management structure

- Members are typically agency administrators, executives, or their designees from involved agencies/organizations

- A MAC group will act as a policy-level body making cooperative decisions and supporting resource prioritization and allocation

- Policy only, they do not perform an incident command functions or replace an EOC

---

## Joint Information System (JIS)

- Combines public affairs and incident information into a single organization

- Provides information to the public and stakeholders

- Located in a JIC (Joint Information Center)

  - Most incidents should only have a single JIC

- Includes the PIO (Public Information Officer)

  - May use a different title in an EOC

---

## Joint Information System (JIS)

- Public information communication plans should be included in training and exercises

  - Public information may include lifesaving  measures and evacuation routes

  - Should include draft news releases, media lists, and contact information for officials, leaders, and public/private organizations

---

## Joint Information System (JIS)

### Public Information Officer duties

- Advises the IC, Unified Command, or EOC director on public information matters
- Gathers, verifies, coordinates, and disseminates information in an accurate, accessible, and timely manner
	- Gathers complete information for the public and other stakeholders
	- Verifyies information to ensure accuracy
	- Coordinates information with other public information personnel who are part of the JIS to ensure consistency
	- Disseminates consistent, coordinated, accurate, accessible, timely, and complete information to the public and stakeholders

---

## Joint Information System (JIS)

### Public Information Officer duties

- Handles inquiries from the media, the public, and elected officials
- Provides emergency public information and warnings
- Conducts rumor monitoring and response
